Worlds Beyond

Color Theory

Description

This hands-on project explores how typography and color palette work together to create a cohesive piece, and how multiple pieces can combine to tell an overarching story. Worlds Beyond presents five distinct planets, each with its own typographic and color scheme. They were designed as individual posters, then combined into an accordion book that tells the story of a cosmic traveler.

Design Process

I worked with five different color schemes: analagous, complementary, split complementary, tertiary triad, and monochromatic. Each color scheme was paired with an adjective to create a piece that evokes that word through visuals and typography. I wanted my overarching story to be a traveler journaling his visits to planets on his adventure. Each planet had to evoke its associated adjective through composition, texture, and/or color. I ultimately decided on these combinations:

  • Analogous: blue, blue-violet, violet
  • Complementary: yellow, violet
  • Split Complementary: red-orange, yellow-orange, blue
  • Tertiary Triad: yellow-orange, blue-green, red-violet
  • Monochromatic: blue

Prague Astronomical Clock Poster

Information Design

Description

The world is filled with interesting landmarks, and each has a story to tell. There is data built into every landmark’s story, and that data can be broken down into sets that include materials, location, visitation numbers, etc. This project resulted in both a static 18×24 print piece as well as an interactive mobile mockup of information relating to the astronomical clock at Prague.

Design Process

Following my trip in Europe, I was captivated by the design of the astronomical clock in Prague, Czech. I took this as an opportunity to create a poster that visually teaches viewers different aspects of the clock, from its history originating in 1410 to the different parts of space and time it measures. I took inspiration from the clock's design elements to design the poster background, in both color and form. This clock also tracks the phases of the moon, and I paid homage to that by designing the timeline with stars (denoting 50 year gaps) and a different moon phase for each major event. This also required a mobile app prototype on Figma, which required re-arranging my poster information to fit comfortably on a small screen.

How To Fold a Lotus Flower

Information Design

Description

In an age where video tutorials dominate, how can information design offer something more universal and accessible? This project answers that question through a step-by-step interactive infographic showing users how to fold an origami lotus flower. Two deliverables were produced: a mobile and desktop interactive prototype. The challenge was to show rather than tell, and let illustrations carry the explanation while keeping text at a minimum.

Design Process

I wanted to detail every step of creating an origami piece as accurately and cleanly as possible. To show, not tell, I added in supplementary text only as an optional drop down for each step. The infographics and the legend help guide the user instead of relying on purely reading. Both prototypes were made on Figma, complete with a scrollable interface and interactive buttons.

Visualizing Google's History

Information Design

Description

Timelines are important pieces of information design that offer snapshots of historical data in a digestible, visually appealing way. This project presents a timeline of Google's history with embedded data sets including product launches and leadership changes. This timeline was designed as a static 11×17 print piece, balancing information density with clear visual hierarchy.

Design Process

Traditional timelines are often read from left to right or up to down following a straight line. But since Google's logo is a big uppercase G, I thought it would be fitting to create a timeline based on the motion of this character G instead. I didn't just have to document Google's big events on this timeline, I also had to add in other aspects like their stock history or their leadership changes. G's base shape is a circle, and I thought, why not make concentric circle timelines inside each other? This approach will show both what events occurred for each data category but also the correlation between data categories. Did one CEO push the company to create more products during their leadership? Did the release of a certain product correlate with a big change in stock share prices? These are the types of questions I wanted to visualize the answers to in my complex timeline poster.

Personal Brand Identity and Logo Design

Brand Design

Description

Defining who you are is the first step to setting yourself apart from others. This is a personal logo project that combines my initials "AZ" with moon imagery to form a clear visible logo. The final mark works in color, black and white, and reverse, and stays legible from any large format down to 3/8".

Design Process

For this project, I designed a personal logo and visual identity meant to communicate my design elegance through crescents and circles. I started with rapid sketching, producing more than 70 thumbnail concepts in the forms of monograms, wordmarks, and symbolic marks. Through repeated critique sessions, I narrowed these down to six digital comps in Illustrator, then refined the three strongest. I developed typographic lockups, tested how small the logo could go while staying legible (3/8"), and explored color iterations. The logo I went with in the end utilized typographic lockup to create a seamless connection between the two letters of my initial, while the moon sits cleanly next to the typography and provides visual direction and asymmetry. The final brand identity deliverable includes black & white, color, and reverse versions, delivered as high quality production-ready vector files.
